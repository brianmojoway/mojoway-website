MOJOWAY / On starting

84% of coaching clients are happy. Only 35% ever hire a coach.

The gap isn't information, and it isn't quality. It's the moment before you begin, and who earns money when you do.

A note on why we built the matching side the way we did

The International Coaching Federation published four numbers in 2026. 74% of people know what coaching is. 56% have considered hiring a coach. 35% have actually done it. And of the people who did, 84% said they were satisfied.

I keep coming back to the distance between 56 and 35. Twenty-one points of people who thought about it and stopped. Since satisfaction sits at 84, whatever stopped them was not a bad experience. Most never got far enough to have one.

There's a caveat worth stating, because it makes the number more interesting rather than less. The 84% is drawn from people who already crossed the line. They arrived with a question clear enough to bring into a room. People who are determined enough to start tend to do well once they do. So the high satisfaction figure tells us the service works for those who get there. It says nothing about the twenty-one points who didn't.

A directory can't fix this

When someone can't name what they want help with, the usual answer is to give them more options. Coach directories list thousands of profiles. One of the larger ones has more than 15,000. You can filter by specialty, credential, language, price.

That solves a problem for the person who already knows they want a career coach and wants to compare five. It doesn't solve anything for the person who is still turning over a vague sense that something keeps repeating. Handing them 15,000 names adds a second decision on top of the first. Now they have to choose a person before they've chosen a question.

I've watched this happen outside coaching too. Give someone a menu when they're hungry and it's useful. Give them a menu when they can't yet say what they're hungry for and they close it.

The commission problem

Most coach marketplaces charge the coach in two ways: a subscription, and a cut of each engagement. The larger directory's Pro plan is $447 a year plus 30% of what is billed through it; the Elite plan is $1,164 a year plus 15%.

Run three clients at $1,500 each through the Pro plan and the coach pays roughly $1,797 for that year. On a flat subscription with no commission, the same year costs about $708. The difference is $1,089.

I want to be careful here. Charging a commission is not dishonest. It's an ordinary way to run a marketplace, and plenty of good platforms use it. What I'm interested in is what that fee does to the product over time.

A platform that earns only when a transaction closes will build tools for closing transactions. Ranking, nudges, "coaches you might also like", the reminder that your shortlist is waiting. Every one of them points toward yes. There is no revenue in "maybe you're not ready yet", so there is no feature for it either. Nobody decides to make the product worse. The incentive just quietly chooses which parts get built.

The fee isn't the problem. The shape of the product the fee produces is.

What we did differently, and what we didn't

We don't take a cut of the booking. A coach pays for the workspace, and what they earn from a client stays with them. That sounds like a pricing detail. It changes the ranking.

With no commission, there's no reason to put one coach above another. The list is ordered by when each coach next has an open slot, and that's it. No ratings, no "top rated" badge, no paid placement, no count of how many clients someone has closed. We show three people, and each one comes with a single sentence that refers back to your own read, so you can see why that person rather than another. If there aren't five prior sessions to justify a statistical sentence, we say what we can actually verify about the fit instead of inventing a number.

The first session is free and short. That promise needs a footnote: a coach with an empty week gives up almost nothing by offering thirty minutes, while a coach who is fully booked gives up real income. So free slots vary with how busy someone is. It's a small operational detail, and it's exactly the kind of detail that decides whether the idea survives contact with real calendars.

I haven't found many products that put these pieces together in this order: read yourself first, then get connected, with no commission on the connection. Plenty of tools do assessments and stop there. Plenty of directories do matching and start there. Joining the two ends without earning from the transaction is less common. That's an observation from the products I've looked at, not a claim that we're the only ones doing it.

Why the order matters

If you match first, you choose by credentials, by a headshot, by who sounds most authoritative. If you read first, you choose by something closer to "this is the thing I keep circling". Those produce different conversations in the first ten minutes.

Our read is deliberately narrow. It describes patterns in how you behave. It doesn't diagnose, doesn't give you a score, doesn't use health data, doesn't tell you what will happen next. What we call core energy is a behaviour pattern, not a reading of vitality or wellbeing. It isn't open to anyone under 18. And what you write in a conversation stays yours by default: a coach sees only the parts you choose to share.

Those constraints read like legal boilerplate. They aren't. They're the reason someone can be honest in the first place. A tool that scores you gets a performance. A tool that grades your health gets a guarded answer. A tool that hands your words to a stranger before you've agreed gets nothing much at all.

This is also where the three parts stop being three separate features. A person who has seen their own pattern has something to bring. A coach who has three concrete places to open from can meet them there. A connection that isn't paid per sale has no reason to rush either of them. Take the commission out and the whole thing can stay slow enough to be useful.

Where we actually are

I should be straight about this, because you can go and check. The booking and confirmation plumbing is live. We ran 29 end-to-end checks on putting a slot up, booking it, confirming it happened, and recording it. They passed.

No coach has put a slot up yet. No session has taken place. If you open the coaches page today it says, truthfully, that nobody has open slots right now. An empty page is a bad destination for a piece of writing, so this article is not going to send you there. I'd rather say that plainly than write around it.

We are building the connection side in public, and the supply side comes first. The first coach who hangs a real slot on the page matters more to this idea than any amount of traffic.

A smaller first step

There is a free read you can do without an account, and it stores nothing. See whether it names something you've been circling but haven't quite said out loud. If it does, you already have a first question to bring to a coach, whenever you decide to bring one. That's a better starting point than picking a person from a list.

And if it doesn't, that's useful too. You've learned something about what you're not looking for, and you haven't spent anything to find out.
