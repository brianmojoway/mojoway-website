Leadership Presence Through the Five Energies

2026-11-05 · 7 min read · by Brian Zhang

Ask ten coaches what executive presence means and you'll get ten variations on the same theme: the room goes quiet when they speak, people trust them, they seem steady under pressure. Useful descriptions, but they all point at the same narrow picture. The deep voice, the unhurried delivery, the confident silence. That picture works beautifully for some leaders and quietly fails others.

In leadership coaching, the more useful question is not whether a leader matches the mould but what kind of presence their energy naturally creates, and how to help them inhabit it fully. The five elements give us a vocabulary for that. Wood presence moves things forward. Fire presence warms a room. Earth presence holds it steady. Metal presence cuts through. Water presence reads the current. Each is a real form of presence. The coaching work is to help a leader stop imitating someone else's and start showing up as their own, deliberately.

Why the single mould fails good leaders

Most executive presence feedback is really a request to be more like the person giving the feedback. A calm, deliberate senior stakeholder tells a fast-moving product director to slow down and speak less. The advice is well meant and often lands badly, because it asks the leader to suppress the very quality that got them the role.

When coaching with the five elements, we treat presence as a matter of range and timing rather than a fixed style. A Wood leader who learns when to pause becomes more effective, not less themselves. A Water leader who learns when to state a view plainly keeps their perceptiveness and adds decisiveness. The aim is not to sand down the edges. It is to give each leader a wider band of expression they can choose from.

Wood: presence as direction

Wood energy is growth, decision and forward motion. Wood leaders create presence by giving people a clear sense of where things are going and why. When they are at their best, the room feels oriented. When they are under strain, the same energy becomes impatience, and presence turns into pressure.

Coaching Wood presence usually means working on pace and listening. The question to explore is not how to be calmer but how to make the direction visible without pushing. A useful practice is to state the destination and then ask a genuine question, letting the answer change the plan.

Worked example: a VP of operations kept getting feedback that she was 'intense' in leadership meetings. Rather than coaching her to soften, we looked at where her urgency came from. She was moving fast because she could see a bottleneck nobody else had named. Her new approach was to open with the bottleneck, then hand the room the problem. Same drive, different delivery. Within a quarter, peers described her as focused rather than forceful.

Fire: presence as warmth and clarity

Fire energy is connection, visibility and expression. Fire leaders create presence through warmth and the ability to make people feel seen. They are often the ones who remember the detail someone mentioned last month. Under pressure, Fire can tip into over-explaining, seeking reassurance, or filling every silence.

The coaching edge for Fire is containment: staying warm while tolerating quiet, and letting clarity carry more weight than volume. Presence for a Fire leader is often about what they leave out.

Worked example: a sales director was told she 'dominated' client meetings. In a recorded review, she saw that she answered her own questions before anyone else could. The shift was small and specific: ask, then count to five. Her clients started bringing more to the table, and her warmth read as confidence rather than nervousness.

Earth: presence as steadiness

Earth energy is grounding, reliability and care. Earth leaders create presence by being the person others can lean on. In a crisis, people look for them. Under strain, Earth can become over-responsible, slow to decide, or reluctant to let a difficult conversation happen.

Coaching Earth presence is often about boundaries and decisiveness. The leader already has the trust. The work is to use it, to name a hard truth or make a call without carrying everyone's feelings as their own.

Worked example: a head of people was admired for how she held her team through a restructure, but her director noticed she avoided the conversation about redundancies until the last possible week. We rehearsed the conversation and named the fear underneath it, which was letting people down. She delivered the news earlier and more plainly. Her team described her afterwards as even more trustworthy, not less kind.

Metal and Water: presence as discernment and depth

Metal energy is precision, standards and discernment. Metal leaders create presence through clarity and the courage to name what is not working. Under pressure, Metal can become cold or overly critical. The coaching work is to keep the standard and add the human context. A useful practice is to pair every piece of critique with a specific acknowledgement.

Water energy is perception, adaptability and depth. Water leaders create presence by reading the room and saying the thing nobody else has said. Under strain, Water can become indirect or hard to read. The coaching work is to make the insight visible, to say the clear sentence rather than circling it.

Worked example: a Metal finance lead was respected but rarely invited into strategy conversations. We worked on how he opened. Instead of leading with the gap in the plan, he began with what the plan got right, then named the risk in one sentence. Same discernment, different entry point. He was in the next three strategy sessions.

Worked example: a Water founder was known for sensing tension in the team but never naming it directly. We practised one sentence: 'I notice we're not saying the real thing here.' Said once, in a calm moment, it changed the tone of the whole leadership team.

  • Wood: name the direction, then ask a real question
  • Fire: ask, then leave five seconds of quiet
  • Earth: make the call you have already earned the trust to make
  • Metal: lead with what works, then name the risk in one sentence
  • Water: say the clear sentence instead of circling it

Coaching across types without forcing a mould

The practical takeaway for coaches is to resist the urge to correct a leader towards the dominant culture's idea of presence. Instead, ask three questions in the coaching session. What does this leader's presence already do well? Where does it tip into strain? And what is the smallest behavioural shift that would widen their range without asking them to be someone else?

This is where the five elements earn their keep. They give you a shared language for qualities that are hard to name in the moment, and they keep the conversation about range rather than personality. A leader does not need to become Earth if they are Wood. They need to know when to borrow a little steadiness, and to trust that their own energy is a legitimate way to lead.

Presence, coached this way, stops being a performance standard and becomes a practice of self-knowledge. That is a more durable thing to build, and a kinder one to ask of anyone.
