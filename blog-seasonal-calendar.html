A Five Energies Calendar for Coaches: Plan with Seasons, Not Against Them

2026-10-01 · 5 min read · by Brian Zhang

Most coaching calendars look like a game of Tetris. You cram sessions into whatever white space is left after client calls, admin, and life. Then you wonder why some weeks feel like wading through treacle while others fly. The problem isn't your time management. It's that you're treating every week as if it should feel the same.

The MOJOWAY five energies framework offers a different way. It maps five elemental qualities, Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water, onto a repeating cycle. This isn't a clinical tool or a diagnostic system. It's a reflection lens. When you use it to shape your coaching calendar, you stop fighting the natural rises and falls of your energy and start working with them.

Why the standard coaching calendar works against you

Coaches are often taught to build a predictable week. Same number of sessions each day. Same admin blocks. Same marketing hours. That structure works for factories, not for humans. Your capacity shifts with sleep, seasons, client load, and the simple fact that some weeks ask more of you than others.

When you ignore those shifts, you end up overloading your high-energy days and dragging yourself through low ones. A five energies calendar doesn't ask you to work less. It asks you to match the type of work to the energy available. That's a small change with a big effect on how sustainable your practice feels.

Consider a coach in Toronto. In January she scheduled eight client sessions a week, plus a webinar, plus a networking event. By February she was resentful and foggy. She hadn't done anything wrong. She had simply planned a Fire schedule during a Water month.

The five energies as a planning rhythm

Each of the five elements carries a distinct quality. Wood is vision, planning, and forward movement. Fire is connection, visibility, and expression. Earth is grounding, nurturing, and steady follow-through. Metal is refinement, boundaries, and letting go. Water is rest, reflection, and deep listening.

In a five energies calendar, you don't assign one element to a whole month and call it done. You notice which energy is dominant in your life right now, then you shape your work around it. If you're in a Water phase, you protect more space for reflection. If you're in a Fire phase, you lean into visibility and connection.

Here's how that looks in practice. A coach in Melbourne noticed she was in a Metal phase, feeling the need to tighten boundaries and review her client roster. Instead of pushing for new leads, she spent two weeks refining her onboarding process and ending a draining contract. That was the right work for that season. It also made the next Fire phase far more effective.

  • Wood: plan, strategise, start new projects, set goals
  • Fire: connect, market, run workshops, have visible conversations
  • Earth: follow up, nurture clients, complete admin, hold steady
  • Metal: refine, edit, set boundaries, review and release
  • Water: rest, reflect, journal, listen deeply, do less

Building your own five energies calendar

Start by tracking your energy for two weeks. Note when you feel most alert, most social, most inward, and most depleted. Don't judge it. Just gather data. Then look for patterns. You might find that Mondays are naturally Wood, full of ideas and drive. Fridays might be Metal, good for tying up loose ends.

Next, map your client work onto those patterns. Put high-focus sessions in Wood or Fire windows. Put admin and follow-up in Earth windows. Put business development in Fire. Put rest and reflection in Water. This isn't rigid. It's a starting point.

A coach in London tried this and realised she was running all her discovery calls on Friday afternoons, her lowest energy point. She moved them to Tuesday mornings and immediately felt more present. Her conversion rate didn't change overnight, but her experience of the work did. That matters.

Working with seasons, not against them

The five energies also map onto the seasons. Spring is Wood, summer is Fire, late summer is Earth, autumn is Metal, and winter is Water. You can use this larger cycle to plan your quarter or your year. It gives you permission to have a quiet season without calling it failure.

Many coaches try to maintain the same output all year. Then they burn out in November and wonder what went wrong. A seasonal approach says: winter is for reflection and planning, not for launching five new offers. Spring is for planting. Summer is for visibility. Autumn is for harvesting and refining.

This doesn't mean you stop working in winter. It means you adjust the mix. You might keep client sessions steady but pause new projects. You might trade a launch for a review. The goal is a calendar that supports you rather than one you have to survive.

A worked example: one coach's week

Let's take a coach in Chicago. She has ten client sessions, two group calls, and a newsletter each week. Under her old calendar, she spread everything evenly and felt behind by Wednesday. Under a five energies calendar, she grouped her work differently.

Monday morning became Wood: she planned the week, reviewed client goals, and wrote her newsletter draft. Tuesday and Wednesday mornings became Fire: all client sessions and the group calls, when her connection energy was highest. Thursday became Earth: admin, follow-ups, and invoicing. Friday morning became Metal: editing the newsletter, reviewing the week, and setting one boundary for the next week. Friday afternoon became Water: no calls, just reflection or rest.

The total hours didn't change. But her experience of the week did. She stopped dreading Fridays. She stopped feeling guilty about quiet afternoons. And her clients noticed she was more present. That's the point of this framework. It's not about doing more. It's about doing what fits.

Making it stick without rigidity

The biggest risk with any planning system is turning it into another stick to beat yourself with. The five energies calendar is a lens, not a law. Some weeks will be chaotic. Some months will refuse to fit any element. That's fine. The framework is there to help you notice, not to make you feel wrong.

Start small. Pick one change, like protecting a Water hour on Friday afternoon. Try it for three weeks. Notice what happens. Then add another. Over time, you'll build a calendar that reflects your real life, not an idealised version of it.

If you're a coach who wants to explore this with your own clients, the MOJOWAY five energies framework gives you a shared language for rhythm, rest, and season. It's a gentle way to help people plan without shame. And it starts with your own calendar first.
