Reading Client Resistance Through the Five Energies

2026-11-13 · 7 min read · by Brian Zhang

Every coach meets resistance. A client who nods along but never acts. One who argues with every suggestion. Another who goes quiet the moment the work gets close to something real. The usual instinct is to push harder or to back off, but both moves miss the point: resistance is information, not an obstacle to break through.

The five energies give you a fast, practical lens for reading that signal. Instead of labeling a client as difficult, you can notice which energy is over-expressed or under-expressed and adjust your next move. This is not a diagnostic model and it does not put anyone in a box. It is a coaching tool for staying useful when the conversation stalls.

Wood — Resistance as a Blocked Drive to Act

Wood is direction, initiation, and forward movement. When Wood is frustrated, a client often shows resistance as argument, impatience, or a refusal to follow a plan they half-agreed to. The mistake is reading this as defiance; more often it is energy that has nowhere to go.

In the session, meet blocked Wood with a concrete, small step rather than more discussion. Ask what one move they could make before the next call, and let them choose it. When a client feels momentum returning, the resistance usually softens because the energy finally has a channel. Coaches who keep analyzing a stuck Wood client tend to report the same argument repeating week after week.

Fire — Resistance as a Break in Connection

Fire is visibility, warmth, and the felt sense of being seen. Resistance framed as Fire often looks like withdrawal: short answers, humor that deflects, or a sudden change of subject whenever something personal comes up. The client is not refusing the work so much as protecting a raw spot.

The repair is relational before it is technical. Rebuild the connection with a genuine check-in and slow the pace. Name something real you appreciate about their effort. When Fire is restored, the client relaxes into the room and the earlier resistance loses its charge. Skipping this step to chase the agenda almost always deepens the distance.

Earth — Resistance as a Need to Be Held

Earth is stability, care, and the experience of being held. A client living in Earth resistance may seem agreeable on the surface yet never quite engage; they are waiting to feel safe before they invest. Pushing content at them reads as pressure and confirms their caution.

Offer containment first. Let the session be a place that asks little and receives much. A simple question about what has felt heavy to carry can open more than any clever framework. Coaches sometimes mistake this holding for lost time, but the client who feels steady in your presence is the one who later takes the bigger risks in the work.

Metal — Resistance as a Clash of Values

Metal is discernment, worth, and what a person is willing to let go of. Resistance here shows up as principled refusal: the client pushes back not because they are stuck but because a suggestion violates something they hold as true. Treat this as data about their values, not as non-compliance.

Name the value you think is speaking and check if you have it right. When the client feels understood on that level, cooperation returns without you having to win the point. The fastest way to harden Metal resistance is to keep advocating for a path the client has quietly decided is wrong for them.

Water — Resistance as a Fear of the Deep

Water is depth, rest, and the slow turn toward meaning. A client resisting from Water will often intellectualize, tell polished stories, or keep the session safely abstract. The work stays near the surface because going lower feels risky.

Protect the quiet. Ask one open question and let it sit without filling the silence. The discomfort you feel as a coach is often the exact edge the client needs to approach. When you trust the pause, the client frequently finds the words themselves. Rushing to fill Water resistance with activity only confirms that the deep is off limits.

A Quick Map to Keep at Your Side

Before the next session where you expect pushback, pick the energy you think is most active and write one sentence about how you will respond to it. Wood gets a step. Fire gets connection. Earth gets holding. Metal gets respect for values. Water gets silence.

This takes two minutes and changes the shape of the conversation. Resistance stops being a problem to solve and becomes a signal that tells you where the client actually is. Over a few weeks you will start reading it in the first two minutes instead of after three stalled sessions.
