The trainee who asked the right question

Field note, coaching apprenticeship

Her notebook was full of the wrong things. Which axis, which camera, which output format, which chart. All of it necessary, none of it the job.

In week five she stood next to a client reading a twelve-page scan and asked the only question that mattered: what do I say first.

The senior coach didn't answer with a script. She asked her to look at the three numbers that had moved and pick one sentence a nervous person could hear without flinching.

They spent twenty minutes on that sentence. It was the best twenty minutes of her apprenticeship.

The reason it matters is that a client's first thirty seconds determine whether the next six months happen. If the opening is a data dump, the client starts managing their own face. If it's one honest sentence, they stay in the conversation.

Most coaching education trains the technical layer because it's testable. The opening sentence is where the value is and nobody tests it.

After that day her reports changed shape. They got shorter and they got used.

MOJOWAY's body kit front-loads that exercise: the first line of every page is a sentence about what changed, written for someone who is about to feel something.
