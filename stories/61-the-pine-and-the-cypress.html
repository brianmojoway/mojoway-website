The pine and the cypress

Analects 9.28, Confucius, 5th century BCE

Confucius said: only when the year turns cold do we know that the pine and the cypress are the last to lose their leaves.

It's seven characters, and it's the most consoling sentence in the Analects, which is odd for a book of rules.

He isn't promising that the pine will be comfortable. The pine is in the same winter as everything else. The cypress gets the same wind, the same frozen ground, the same short daylight. What's different is that the winter doesn't change its nature.

The people I've seen hold up best in bad years are rarely the most optimistic ones. They're the ones who were already fairly honest about themselves before the bad year arrived. Losing a job doesn't turn them into somebody new. It costs them something and they carry on being who they were, at a lower temperature.

That's what the metaphor is actually measuring. Not strength. Continuity.

So the useful question in a good season isn't "how do I get ahead". It's "would this version of me still be recognizable if the year turned cold".

Because the year turns cold eventually, and the answer to that question is the only thing you get to keep.

MOJOWAY asks it when things are still warm. That's the only time the answer can be worked with, which is why weekly self-awareness matters more in easy weeks than hard ones.
