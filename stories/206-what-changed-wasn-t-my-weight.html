What changed wasn't my weight

Client account, recorded October 2026

My weight moved eight kilos in two years, which sounds impressive and is the least interesting part of it.

What changed is that I can carry two bags of shopping up the hill from the car park without stopping. That I stopped making excuses about the stairs at work. That I can get off the floor without using my hands, which at fifty-two is apparently a milestone.

None of that was in my goals. My goal was a number. The report I got every quarter had the number in it, and it also had a short list of what my coach had noticed: the shopping, the stairs, the floor thing.

Reading those back at the next review was the first time I understood that the number was the shadow and the list was the thing.

I've since told my coach to keep writing the list. It's the part I care about, and it's the part no machine generates — the machine gives you the figure and the coach gives you the Tuesday.

The other effect is social. I now describe my progress in ways other fifty-two-year-olds recognize instantly, which is a better conversation than the one about kilos.

MOJOWAY's pages hold both: the measured number and the coach's plain-language observation of what it did to someone's week.
