The sandpiper and the clam

Stratagems of the Warring States (战国策), Yan section 2, 3rd century BCE

A clam came out of the water to open its shell in the sun. A sandpiper went for the meat. The clam snapped shut and held the bird's beak.

The bird said: it won't rain today, it won't rain tomorrow, and then you'll dry up on the sand.

The clam said: I won't be out of the water today, I won't be out tomorrow, and then you'll still be here with your beak stuck in a shell.

Neither would move. A fisherman walked up and took both of them.

The story is usually told about how, eh, cooperation beats conflict. That reading is a bit comfortable for what actually happens, which is that the cost of winning was losing.

Look closely and both parties were making a correct forecast. The clam genuinely would have dried out. The bird genuinely would have starved. Both had accurate information, and both were using that information to justify waiting.

That's the pattern to watch in yourself. It's not that you're wrong about the other person. It's that you can be exactly right about them and still be sitting on the beach with your beak in a shell.

Before continuing a fight, get specific about the thing you want, as opposed to the thing you want them to admit.
