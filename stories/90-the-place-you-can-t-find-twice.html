The place you can't find twice

Tao Yuanming, "The Peach Blossom Spring" (桃花源记), 5th century CE

A fisherman from Wuling was drifting up a stream when he came to a grove of peach trees in full flower. He kept going, and the tunnel at the end of the grove opened onto a valley with houses, fields, dogs, chickens, and people.

They had fled the wars centuries before and never left. They asked him news of the outside. They took turns feeding him. They had no idea what dynasty it was.

After a few days he left. On the way out he made marks to remember the way.

He got home and told the magistrate, who sent men to follow the marks. They got lost. Nobody found the place again.

Later a man named Liu Ziji heard the story and went to look. He found nothing, and then he died of an illness. After that nobody tried.

That last part is what makes it a Chinese story rather than a Western one. No returning hero with proof. Just a report, and it stops.

The question it leaves is not whether the village is real. It's whether the marks work. The fisherman tried to make the experience portable, and the attempt to make it portable is exactly what dissolved it.

Some things you find by accident and can't be repeated on purpose. A friendship that formed because two people were stuck in the same town in the same year. A period of your life when the work was easy. A state of mind you keep trying to engineer and only meets you when you're not looking.

MOJOWAY doesn't promise a return to the village. It keeps a record of the terrain, which is the only thing that can be carried back.
