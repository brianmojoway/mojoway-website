The rice held level

*Book of the Later Han* (后汉书·梁鸿传), 5th century CE

Liang Hong was a scholar who had values about not living on other people's wealth. He married a woman named Meng Guang.

They lived in the household of a patron and both of them declined to eat meals that had been earned through another person's corrupt connections. When it became impossible to stay consistent with that, they moved to the mountains and farmed.

The detail that survived: when she served his meals, she would hold the tray up to the level of her own eyebrows, and he would receive it with equal care.

Now, two thousand years of retellings have made this about a wife being deferential. If you read the same passage, something else is going on. Both of them are holding something up. The courtesy is mutual, and mutual courtesy between people who are the same size.

I think that's the actual content. Relationships turn toxic less through big cruelties than through the slow erosion of the small courtesies, which are the visible form of respect.

You know this from the people you've lived with. When did you last hold something up to the level of the other person's eyes, metaphorically, in the way you looked at them while they were talking.

That's not a politeness tip. It's a measurement. When the small courtesy goes, the relationship is usually two years behind it and nobody has said so.
