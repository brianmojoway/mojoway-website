Teaching judgment, not protocol

Field note, coaching education

New coaches arrive wanting the sequence: which exercise first, which cue, which numbers to reference. Protocols are the easy part and they're why the first month feels manageable.

Then a client cries in week six, or gets a diagnosis, or stops coming, and the protocol has nothing to say.

The studios that develop coaches well do one thing differently: they make judgment visible. When a senior coach handles a hard session, the junior coach doesn't watch the exercises, they read the report afterward and try to reconstruct the decisions.

Why that figure and not the other one. Why the coach put the worry in the second paragraph instead of the first. Why the closing line was a question.

You can't teach that with a slide, but you can teach it with artifacts, because artifacts hold decisions that were once invisible.

A new coach who reads a hundred signed reports from their own studio has effectively attended a hundred hours of supervision they weren't in the room for.

MOJOWAY's body kit keeps those reports in a consistent shape, which is what makes them readable as a curriculum rather than as a pile of individual styles.
