The letter he nearly didn't send

Sima Qian, "Letter in Reply to Ren An" (报任安书), 93 BCE

Sima Qian was the court historian when he intervened on behalf of a general who had surrendered to the enemy. The emperor was furious and sentenced him to death.

In Han China, a man facing execution could pay to have it commuted, or accept castration and live. Money was short. He chose the second.

He wrote a letter about it years later to a friend, and described what it cost him. He says he was mocked in his own circle, that he lost the ability to stand in company, and that in a hundred generations people would still say his name with contempt.

And then the reason, which is the sentence that keeps his name alive. He had a book to finish, and finishing it mattered more than the shame. He lists all the writers before him who had been broken and had written anyway, and says he accepts the worst of his own reputation so the work could exist.

The Records of the Grand Historian is still the standard history of ancient China. It was written by a man who had decided that the only way to keep his life was to lose his standing.

We tell the version of the good outcome. The letter is the version where he couldn't see the outcome, only the next page, and chose it with the humiliation of a lifetime already on the table.

That's the part that applies to anybody making something. Not the triumph. The willingness to be misunderstood for years by the people whose opinion you actually care about.

The book gets written by the person who shows up after the humiliation, not the one who avoids it.

MOJOWAY is built on the assumption that the inside story is the true one, and most people never get to say it. A file where a person can be honest about what they're paying, quietly, week after week, is a very small thing to build. It's also the only one I know of that leads anywhere.
