What the churn list doesn't say

Field note, studio owner in Singapore, October 2026

Every studio keeps a list of clients who left. His had forty-one names on it and one column that mattered: date joined, date gone, average months.

He called twenty of them. Not to win them back, just to ask. He expected price, schedule, a move across town. He got something else, over and over: "I couldn't tell if it was working."

One woman said she'd kept her printed scans in a drawer and never looked at them again, because the numbers didn't mean anything to her and nobody had translated.

That was the whole diagnosis. Not value, not price, not competition. Memory. People can't sustain something they can't see the shape of.

He changed two things. Clients now get a short written review every quarter, in plain language, with the starting number next to the current one. And the coach signs it, because a document with a name on it is harder to file away and forget.

The churn list is shorter this year. He doesn't think it's because the training got better. He thinks people finally know what they're getting.

MOJOWAY's body kit was built for that quarterly page: the numbers turned into a few sentences a client can actually read, with room for the coach's own voice.
