A thousand miles

Laozi, *Daodejing*, chapter 64, c. 4th century BCE

Laozi wrote: the journey of a thousand miles begins beneath your feet. Or in the classical line: a journey of a thousand li begins at your feet.

This is the most quoted sentence in the book and therefore the most ignorable. Everyone nods, nobody changes anything, because nobody's problem is "I don't know where to start". Everyone's problem is that the start is too small to feel like it's doing anything.

That's the actual obstacle. Not the beginning. The two weeks after the beginning, when the distance is still a thousand miles and you've walked two.

Laozi's chapter is more interesting than the quote. He talks about not breaking things by grabbing them, about treating the difficult as an accumulation of small things that get done before anyone thinks they're happening.

So the practice isn't "start". It's "make the start small enough that you'll still be doing it in week three".

One page, one client, one walk, ten minutes, the same time every day. Not because it's the fastest route, but because it's the only kind of route that survives contact with a normal life.

Whatever it is for you, cut it in half and do that version tomorrow.
