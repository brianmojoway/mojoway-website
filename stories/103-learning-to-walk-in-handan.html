Learning to walk in Handan

Zhuangzi, chapter 17, "Autumn Floods" (秋水), 4th century BCE

A young man from Shouling went to Handan, the capital of Zhao, because the people there walked beautifully. He went to learn the way they walked.

He studied. He practised. He couldn't get the Handan walk, and when he tried to go back to his own, he found he couldn't do that either.

So he crawled home on his hands and knees.

The standard reading is about not imitating others. I think the interesting part is the sequence: he didn't fail because Handan was bad. He failed because the study replaced the habit before it was replaced by a new habit.

That gap is where most people get stuck. You take a course on selling, and for three months you can't sell the old way and can't yet sell the new way. You read a book about a better way to say no, and now every request gets an awkward pause that satisfies nobody.

The danger isn't learning something new. It's learning it at a pace where you dismantle the old before the new holds weight.

If you're mid-transition, that's normal. Give the new thing time to become automatic before judging it.

MOJOWAY tracks the change across months rather than moments. Progress that looks like going backwards is usually a halfway-built habit, and knowing that is half the endurance.
