The small thing that eats the big thing

Analects 15.27, Confucius, 5th century BCE

Confucius said: if you can't endure the small, you'll ruin the great design.

This is usually heard as advice about patience in arguments, and it is, but the more common version in ordinary life is about the small irritations you keep handling badly.

The meeting where you had to be right about a detail that didn't matter, and now the working relationship has a cold spot in it. The 11 p.m. message sent to settle something small, and the week spent managing the aftermath. The comment in front of colleagues that cost you a sponsorship you never knew you were being considered for.

None of those were the plan. All of them restructured the plan.

What I've noticed in people who are good at this: they treat the small provocations as a category, not as individual events. When the thing arrives, they don't ask "is this worth the fight". They ask "is this a fight at all". The 0.4% of it that's actually a decision gets dealt with, and the rest is weather.

That's a skill and it's trainable, but only if you're honest about which small things actually get you. For most people it isn't the big shocking insult. It's being corrected in front of someone, or being disorganised in public, or being late and being seen to be late.

You know yours. Everyone knows theirs. The question is what you're willing to do in the two seconds after it happens.
