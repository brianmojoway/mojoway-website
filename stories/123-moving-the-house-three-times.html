Moving the house three times

*Biographies of Exemplary Women* (列女传), "Mother of Mencius", 1st century BCE

Mencius's father died when he was small. His mother raised him alone and they lived near a graveyard.

The boy began playing at funerals. He'd arrange stones and imitate the wailing and the bowing. His mother saw it and said: this is no place to raise a child. They moved.

They went to live near a market. The boy began playing at trade, imitating the vendors calling prices, the haggling, the counting.

His mother said: this is no place to raise a child either. They moved again, next to a school.

The boy began playing at ritual and courtesy, bowing, arranging things in order. His mother said: this is where my son belongs. They stayed.

The story is famous as an argument for environment, and it's usually told in a way that makes the mother fussy. What strikes me is her criterion. She wasn't measuring the neighbourhood's status or its safety. She was watching what her son practised when he thought nobody was evaluating him.

That's a better test than any list of good environments. What does the person become automatic about here.

Apply it to yourself and to the rooms you put yourself in. Your group chat, your team, the people you eat lunch with. Not what they say they value. What you find your own voice doing after a few months.
