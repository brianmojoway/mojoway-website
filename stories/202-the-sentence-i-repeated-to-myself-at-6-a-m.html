The sentence I repeated to myself at 6 a.m.

Client account, recorded October 2026

I train at six, which means at 5:40 I'm in a kitchen deciding whether to be a person who trains.

The line I use is from a review last winter: "Your energy on training days has been consistently better than non-training days for four months." My coach wrote it as an observation. I read it as instructions.

It's not motivational language. It's a summary of my own data that happens to answer the exact question I ask at 5:40 in the morning, which is whether this does anything.

I've noticed that most fitness content is written for the version of me that is motivated, on a Sunday, with a coffee. Nobody writes for the 6 a.m. version, who needs a fact rather than a slogan.

The other line I use is about the shoulder: "No flare-ups in eleven weeks." I read that on mornings when something twinges, to remind myself that the twinge isn't the trend.

A quarterly document sounds like the least motivating artifact imaginable. In practice a page that says something true about my body is the only thing I've ever repeated to myself.

MOJOWAY's reviews put the coach's observation in the client's own language, which is why mine survives the trip from the page into my head.
