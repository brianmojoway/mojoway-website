Adding by subtracting

Laozi, *Daodejing*, chapter 48, c. 4th century BCE

Laozi wrote: in the pursuit of learning, one accumulates daily. In the pursuit of the Way, one decreases daily.

Both things are true at once and most people only ever do the first.

You accumulate courses, frameworks, notes, bookmarks, tactics. Every one of them is a genuine improvement, and the total set is now so large that no single one can be practised long enough to do anything. The knowledge is real and it has become a form of clutter.

The subtraction isn't about reading less. It's about letting one useful thing through while the others queue.

I've watched coaches with twenty frameworks use none of them, and coaches with one framework use it with such fluency that clients cry. The difference wasn't information.

The chapter continues: you subtract and subtract again, until you find yourself doing nothing, and then everything is done. That last part is obviously not literal. It describes the feeling of a skill that has gone quiet.

The practical version is a page with two columns. On the left, everything you're currently trying to improve. On the right, one thing. The right column is the year.

MOJOWAY is deliberately one thing: a client, a conversation, a pattern. The constraint is the feature, and the clarity it produces is where self-awareness stops being homework.
