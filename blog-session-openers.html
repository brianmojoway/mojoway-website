Three Opening Questions for Every Element in Coaching

2026-09-30 · 5 min read · by Brian Zhang

The first five minutes of a coaching session often set the tone for the next fifty. Too many coaches default to 'What do you want to talk about today?' and then spend twenty minutes circling the real issue. But what if you could open with a question that meets your client exactly where they are, according to the energy they bring into the room? That is where the MOJOWAY Five Elements framework becomes a practical tool for session design.

MOJOWAY is a personal reflection framework based on Daoist Five Elements: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water. It is not a diagnostic instrument, and it does not replace your coaching training. It is simply a lens for noticing which energy is most present in your client right now. When you match your opening question to that energy, you help the client name what matters faster and with less resistance. Below are three core questions, adapted for each element, with a worked example for each.

The Three Core Questions

Before adapting to the elements, start with three simple, powerful questions that work across any coaching conversation. First: What is most alive for you right now? Second: What would make this session a success? Third: What is the one thing you are not saying? These questions are open, non-leading, and invite the client to self-generate the agenda.

The magic is not in the questions themselves but in how you tune them to the client's current element. A Wood client might need a question that invites forward motion, while a Water client might need one that allows for reflection. The adaptation is not about changing the question's intent, but its flavor.

  • What is most alive for you right now?
  • What would make this session a success?
  • What is the one thing you are not saying?

Wood Energy: Questions for Growth and Direction

Wood energy is about vision, growth, and pushing forward. Clients in Wood often arrive with a plan or a frustration about obstacles. They want to move. Your opening question should channel that drive without adding pressure. Instead of 'What do you want to talk about?', try 'What is the next step you are most ready to take?' or 'What would make this session a success in terms of forward motion?'

For the third core question, adapt it to 'What is the one thing you are not saying that could unlock your next move?' This honors Wood's need for action while gently surfacing what might be blocking it. Avoid questions that feel too abstract or reflective, which can frustrate a Wood client.

Worked example: A product manager in a tech startup comes to coaching feeling stuck on a decision about whether to leave her job. She is leaning forward, talking fast, and has a list of pros and cons. Instead of asking 'How do you feel about it?', you open with 'What is the next step you are most ready to take?' She pauses, then says, 'I need to have a conversation with my manager about what I actually want.' The session now has a clear direction, and you can explore what that conversation would look like.

Fire Energy: Questions for Passion and Connection

Fire energy is about joy, connection, and emotional expression. Clients in Fire often arrive animated, maybe even scattered, with lots of ideas and feelings. They need a question that helps them focus their enthusiasm without dampening it. Try 'What is most alive for you right now, and what would make this session a success in terms of connection?'

For the third question, adapt to 'What is the one thing you are not saying that you are most excited or worried about?' This invites Fire clients to channel their emotional energy into a specific insight. Avoid questions that feel too cold or analytical, which can shut down their natural warmth.

Worked example: A sales director comes to coaching after a big win. She is excited but also exhausted. She talks about the team, the client, the celebration. You ask, 'What is most alive for you right now?' She says, 'I am proud, but I am also worried I am burning out.' That opens a deeper conversation about sustainable pace, which she had not planned to discuss. The first five minutes uncovered the real issue.

Earth Energy: Questions for Stability and Care

Earth energy is about nurturing, practicality, and groundedness. Clients in Earth often arrive calm but may be carrying others' burdens. They need a question that honors their need for stability while gently inviting them to consider their own needs. Try 'What would make this session a success in terms of feeling more grounded?'

For the third question, adapt to 'What is the one thing you are not saying that you are carrying for someone else?' This can help Earth clients set a boundary or ask for support. Avoid questions that feel too forceful or urgent, which can overwhelm their natural pace.

Worked example: A team lead in a nonprofit comes to coaching after a week of mediating conflicts. She is calm but tired. You ask, 'What would make this session a success in terms of feeling more grounded?' She says, 'I need to stop being everyone's therapist.' That leads to a plan for how she can delegate emotional labor and protect her own energy.

Metal Energy: Questions for Clarity and Release

Metal energy is about clarity, structure, and letting go. Clients in Metal often arrive with a sharp focus on what is not working. They need a question that helps them refine and release. Try 'What would make this session a success in terms of gaining clarity?'

For the third question, adapt to 'What is the one thing you are not saying that you are ready to let go of?' This honors Metal's need for precision and closure. Avoid questions that feel too vague or emotional, which can frustrate a Metal client.

Worked example: A lawyer comes to coaching feeling stuck on a case. He is analytical and wants to solve the problem. You ask, 'What would make this session a success in terms of gaining clarity?' He says, 'I need to stop overworking and trust my team.' That shifts the session from problem-solving to leadership development.

Water Energy: Questions for Reflection and Flow

Water energy is about reflection, intuition, and going with the flow. Clients in Water often arrive quiet or uncertain. They need a question that allows space for emergence. Try 'What would make this session a success in terms of allowing insight to surface?'

For the third question, adapt to 'What is the one thing you are not saying that you are still sitting with?' This gives Water clients permission to explore without pressure. Avoid questions that demand immediate action, which can disrupt their natural process.

Worked example: A writer comes to coaching feeling blocked. She is quiet and says she does not know what to talk about. You ask, 'What would make this session a success in terms of allowing insight to surface?' She pauses, then says, 'I need to stop forcing the writing and just listen.' That opens a conversation about trusting her creative process.
