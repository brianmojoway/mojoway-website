Coaching Onboarding: Make All Five Energies Feel Seen

2026-10-28 · 5 min read · by Brian Zhang

The first ten minutes of a coaching engagement carry more weight than most of us like to admit. Before any goal is set or any plan is made, a client is quietly answering one question: does this person get me? That answer shapes how honest they are, how much they risk, and whether they come back. Coaching onboarding is not paperwork. It is the moment trust either starts to form or quietly stalls.

The MOJOWAY five elements give you a practical lens for that moment. Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal and Water describe five ways people tend to orient toward change. None of them is a personality label, and none of them tells you what a client needs. They simply help you notice how someone is showing up and adjust your first session so they feel met rather than processed. Here is how to onboard each energy in the first ten minutes.

Start with Wood: give the direction room

Wood clients arrive with momentum. They often have a plan, a list, or a strong opinion about what needs to change. In the first ten minutes, the fastest way to lose them is to slow them down with a long intake form or a careful review of your process. They read that as friction. Instead, ask one forward-facing question: what are you moving toward? Then let them talk.

Example: a founder joins a first session and opens with, I need to fix my hiring. Rather than asking about history, you say, tell me what good hiring looks like twelve months from now. She speaks for six minutes. You reflect back the direction you heard. She relaxes, because the session already feels like progress. Wood clients feel seen when their sense of direction is treated as useful information, not as something to be managed.

  • Open with a forward question, not a history question.
  • Reflect their direction back in one clear sentence.
  • Keep process talk short and get to the work quickly.

Meet Fire: match the warmth and name the spark

Fire clients lead with energy and connection. They want to feel that you are in it with them, not observing from a clipboard. In the first ten minutes, warmth is not a nicety. It is the signal that tells them this relationship is real. If you stay neutral and procedural, they may read it as distance and start performing instead of opening up.

Example: a team leader laughs, tells a story about her week, and then says, I am not sure this is the right thing to focus on. You name the spark you heard: the part of that story where your voice changed felt important. She pauses, then goes deeper. Fire clients feel seen when you notice what lights them up and reflect it back without turning it into a task list.

  • Greet with genuine warmth and a little of your own presence.
  • Notice the moment their energy rises and name it.
  • Resist the urge to convert excitement into action items immediately.

Ground Earth: slow down and hold the middle

Earth clients often come in carrying everyone else. They describe the team, the family, the obligations, and only near the end do they mention themselves. In the first ten minutes, the most useful thing you can do is slow the pace and make room. Rushing them toward a goal feels like one more person needing something from them.

Example: a manager spends eight minutes explaining a conflict between two colleagues. You ask, and where are you in that? She stops. That single question tells her the session is about her, not just about the people she is holding. Earth clients feel seen when you make space for their experience without asking them to hurry past it.

  • Speak a little slower and leave longer pauses.
  • Ask one question that turns the focus back to them.
  • Acknowledge the load they are carrying before moving to goals.

Respect Metal: be precise and keep the frame clean

Metal clients notice quality. They arrive with a sharp sense of what is relevant and what is not. In the first ten minutes, vagueness costs you credibility. A rambling welcome or an unclear agenda reads as a lack of care. Precision is a form of respect here, and it is often what helps them relax.

Example: a senior analyst asks directly, what is the structure of this engagement? You give a clear, short answer: three phases, here is what each one asks of you, and here is what I will do. He nods and becomes noticeably more open. Metal clients feel seen when you are exact about the frame, because it tells them you take the work seriously.

  • State the structure and your role in plain terms.
  • Use specific language and avoid filler.
  • Let them ask clarifying questions before you dive in.

Welcome Water: follow the current, do not dam it

Water clients often arrive with the whole picture at once. They move between topics, circle back, and think out loud. In the first ten minutes, a rigid agenda will make them feel interrupted. Water needs room to flow before it settles. Your job is to follow the current and notice where it pools.

Example: a consultant starts with a work problem, drifts into a family story, then returns to the work problem with a clearer question. If you had cut in at minute two, you would have missed the insight. Instead you say, I noticed you came back to the same question from a different angle. What do you see now? Water clients feel seen when you trust their process and reflect the pattern rather than forcing a sequence.

  • Let the first few minutes roam before you focus.
  • Listen for the theme that returns.
  • Reflect the pattern instead of correcting the order.

Put it together in the first ten minutes

You do not need to type your client in the first ten minutes, and you should not try. The five elements are a reflection tool, not a diagnostic one. What you can do is stay curious about which energy is most present and let that shape your pacing, your questions and your warmth. A short client intake that asks how someone prefers to start can help, but your attention in the room matters more than any form.

Example: a coach opens every first session with the same three questions, then adjusts the follow-up based on what she hears. For a Wood client she asks about direction. For an Earth client she asks about load. Same opening, different second move. Over time, clients describe her as someone who gets it quickly. That reputation is built in ten-minute increments, one energy at a time.

  • Keep your opening consistent, then adapt the second question.
  • Name what you notice rather than labeling the person.
  • Review the first ten minutes after each session and note what worked.
