The drift is normal: why clients slide off goals

2026-10-06 · 5 min read · by Brian Zhang

It happens around week three or four. The client who was all fire in the first session starts rescheduling. Their check-in gets shorter. They say things like 'I've been a bit busy' or 'I'll get back on it next week.' As a coach, your instinct might be to push harder, add more accountability, or wonder if you did something wrong. You didn't. This is the drift, and it is a normal part of how humans move through change.

Drift is not failure. It is information. It shows you where the client's energy has shifted, what has become uncomfortable, or what they are quietly protecting. In the MOJOWAY framework, we read that information through five elemental energies: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water. Each energy has a different way of sliding off a goal, and each gives one early signal before the client fully disengages. Knowing that signal helps you respond this week without fixing, diagnosing, or chasing.

Why the first motivated weeks fade

The first weeks of coaching often run on novelty and relief. The client has finally said the thing out loud, made a plan, and felt seen. That produces a natural lift. But novelty is a short fuel. Once the plan meets real life, the old patterns reassert themselves, and the gap between intention and action becomes visible. That gap is not a character flaw. It is the work.

Drift also happens because goals ask people to give something up, not just add something. A client who wants to set boundaries is also giving up being the reliable one. A client who wants to delegate is giving up control. When the cost of the goal becomes felt, the nervous system often chooses the familiar over the desired. The drift is the client negotiating that cost, usually without words.

As a coach, your job is not to eliminate drift. It is to name it early, stay curious, and help the client choose their next step with open eyes. That is where the five energies give you a practical map.

Wood: the early signal is sharper pushing

Wood energy is about direction, growth, and forward motion. When a Wood-type client drifts, they usually do not go quiet. They go harder. They add more goals, more metrics, more urgency. They may start talking about a new plan before the current one has landed. The early signal is a subtle shift from 'I want this' to 'I should already have this.'

Worked example: Maya, a team lead, set a goal to run two workshops this quarter. By week three she had added a newsletter, a podcast idea, and a new certification. Her check-ins were fast and clipped. When asked how the workshops were going, she said, 'Fine, but I'm behind.' The drift was not laziness. It was Wood energy turning a goal into a pressure test.

A practical move this week: slow the pace before adding accountability. Ask, 'What would it look like to do less, but finish one thing?' Then help them pick the smallest next action that still counts. Wood relaxes when direction is clear and the step is finite.

Fire: the early signal is shorter, flatter check-ins

Fire energy is about connection, visibility, and joy. Fire clients drift when the work stops feeling alive or when they sense disappointment, theirs or yours. The early signal is a change in warmth. Messages get shorter. They stop sharing wins. They may say 'all good' in a way that closes the door.

Worked example: Daniel, a sales director, was animated in every session until week four. Then his replies became one line. He stopped sending the small updates he used to send. Nothing was wrong with the plan. The Fire had gone out of the room. When his coach asked, 'What part of this still feels worth doing?' Daniel admitted he missed the team element and had been trying to do it all alone.

A practical move this week: reconnect before you redirect. Share one specific thing you noticed and appreciated. Ask what would make the next step feel more alive. Fire returns through genuine contact, not through more pressure.

Earth: the early signal is 'I'm fine' with no detail

Earth energy is about steadiness, care, and belonging. Earth clients drift by over-giving. They say yes to everyone else and quietly put their own goal last. The early signal is a flat 'I'm fine' when you ask how they are, with no detail and no request. They are still showing up, but they have stopped bringing themselves.

Worked example: Priya, a nonprofit manager, had a goal to protect two mornings a week for deep work. By week three she had given both mornings away. She did not cancel coaching. She arrived tired and agreeable. When asked what she needed, she said, 'I don't want to be a burden.' The drift was not a lack of commitment. It was Earth energy doing what it does: taking care of everyone but the goal.

A practical move this week: give permission before you give a plan. Ask, 'What would you say no to this week so this goal gets one hour?' Then help them rehearse the no. Earth steadies when care is reciprocal, not one-way.

Metal and Water: two more signals to watch

Metal energy is about standards, clarity, and what matters. Metal clients drift when the goal feels sloppy or compromised. The early signal is criticism, often aimed at themselves or at the process. They may say, 'This isn't the right way to do it,' or 'I don't see the point.' That is not resistance for its own sake. It is a request for a cleaner standard.

Worked example: Tom, a partner at a law firm, wanted to improve how he gave feedback. By week three he was rewriting the coaching worksheet and questioning the whole approach. His coach asked, 'What would make this feel worth your time?' Tom named one non-negotiable: it had to be usable in a five-minute hallway conversation. Once that standard was clear, he re-engaged.

Water energy is about rest, depth, and timing. Water clients drift by going quiet and inward. The early signal is vagueness about time: 'soon,' 'later,' 'I'll know when I know.' They are not avoiding you. They are waiting for a clearer internal yes. A practical move for both: ask one precise question and then leave space. 'What is the one thing that would make this feel right?' or 'What do you need before you can move?' Then let the silence do its work.

What to do this week as a coach

You do not need to diagnose anyone. You need to notice the signal and respond to the person in front of you. Drift is not a problem to solve. It is a conversation to open. The five energies give you a way to name what you see without making it mean something about the client's worth or your skill.

Try these moves in your next session:

  • Name the drift plainly and without blame: 'I notice the check-ins got shorter. What's true for you right now?'
  • Ask one energy-specific question instead of offering a fix. Wood: 'What is the one thing that still matters here?' Fire: 'What would make this feel alive again?' Earth: 'What would you say no to?' Metal: 'What standard would make this worth it?' Water: 'What do you need before you move?'
  • Shrink the next step until it is almost too small to resist. One email, one conversation, one hour.
  • Re-contract the goal out loud. Keep it, change it, or pause it. All three are valid and all three reduce quiet drift.

Drift is a doorway, not a dead end

When a client slides off a goal, they are not broken and neither is your coaching. They are showing you where the real work lives. The five energies help you read that moment with more nuance and less panic. You stop chasing momentum and start listening for the signal underneath it.

This week, pick one client who has gone a little quiet or a little sharp. Ask one honest question. Then stay in the room long enough for the real answer. That is often where the coaching actually begins.
