The Month-Three Check-In That Keeps Coaching Clients

2026-09-29 · 5 min read · by Brian Zhang

Month three is where coaching relationships often wobble. The initial excitement has faded, the quick wins are behind you, and the client starts wondering if they still need you. It is not a failure of your coaching. It is a predictable part of the arc. The question is what you do in that moment.

Most coaches either push harder on goals or quietly hope the client renews. Both approaches miss the real opportunity. A short, structured check-in can turn that wobble into a renewal by making the client feel seen, not sold to. This article gives you a five-element check-in you can run in twenty minutes, using the MOJOWAY framework as a reflection tool, not a diagnostic one.

Why month three feels like a wobble

In the first eight weeks, clients are often running on novelty and relief. They have a new person in their corner, a fresh plan, and a sense of momentum. By month three, the novelty has worn off. The work gets harder, results feel slower, and the client starts to wonder whether they are getting value.

This is not a sign that coaching is failing. It is a sign that the relationship is moving from transaction to trust. The client is testing whether you can hold space for the messy middle. If you can, loyalty deepens. If you avoid the conversation, the client often drifts away without ever saying why.

A structured check-in gives you a way to name the wobble without making it a crisis. It shows the client that you are paying attention to them, not just to their goals.

The five-element check-in: a 20-minute structure

The MOJOWAY five elements (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water) offer a simple lens for reflection. They are not a personality test or a clinical tool. They are a way to ask better questions. In this check-in, you move through five short prompts, one for each element, and then close with a forward-looking question.

Wood: What have you been growing or pushing forward this month? Fire: What has felt alive or energising? Earth: What has felt steady or supportive? Metal: What have you let go of or refined? Water: What has felt uncertain or restful?

You do not need to explain the elements to the client unless they ask. You can simply use the questions as a sequence. The point is to cover five different angles of their experience in a short time, so the conversation feels complete rather than scattered.

  • Wood: growth and forward motion
  • Fire: energy and engagement
  • Earth: stability and support
  • Metal: release and refinement
  • Water: uncertainty and rest

How to run the conversation without selling

The biggest mistake coaches make is turning the check-in into a pitch. The moment a client feels sold to, the trust drops. Instead, frame the check-in as a review, not a renewal conversation. Say something like: 'We are at the three-month mark. I would like to spend twenty minutes reflecting on what is working and what needs to shift. No agenda beyond that.'

Then ask the five questions and listen. Take notes. Reflect back what you hear. Only after the client has spoken should you ask: 'What would be most useful for you in the next three months?' That question puts the client in charge of the renewal, not you.

If the client says they are not sure, do not panic. Ask what would need to be true for them to feel confident continuing. Sometimes the answer is a different focus, a different pace, or a shorter commitment. All of those are better than losing the client entirely.

What to do with what you hear

The check-in is only useful if you act on it. If the client says they need more accountability, adjust your sessions. If they say they need less structure, loosen the plan. If they say they are not sure about the value, ask them to describe what value would look like. Then co-create a simple way to track it.

One coach I spoke with uses this check-in every quarter. She told me that month three used to be her biggest drop-off point. Now it is her strongest renewal moment. The difference is not a clever script. It is the willingness to pause and ask.

You can also use the five elements as a shared language over time. After a few check-ins, clients start to notice their own patterns. They might say, 'I have been all Wood and no Water this month.' That self-awareness is often what keeps them engaged.

  • Adjust the plan based on what you hear
  • Co-create a simple value marker
  • Use the elements as a shared language

Turning a wobble into a renewal

A wobble is not a warning sign. It is an invitation. When you meet it with curiosity instead of defensiveness, you show the client that you are there for the long haul. That is what keeps them past month three.

The check-in does not need to be perfect. It needs to be consistent. Put it in your calendar at the three-month mark for every client. Run it the same way each time. Over time, it becomes part of your coaching rhythm, and your clients come to expect it.

Retention is not about locking people in. It is about making the relationship worth staying in. A short, structured check-in is one of the simplest ways to do that.
